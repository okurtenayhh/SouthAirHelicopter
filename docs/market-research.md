## What a Helicopter MRO Site Needs (Category Standards)

**Structural must-haves:**
- Services page(s) broken out by specialty (avionics, structural, engine, hydraulics, inspections)
- Manufacturer/authorization page(s) — which brands they're certified on (biggest trust signal in this industry)
- Visible certifications — FAA repair station number, EASA, OEM authorizations
- Location/heliport info — coordinates or airport code
- Contact/Request a Quote as a repeated, clear CTA (no published pricing — always "contact us")
- About/History — years in business, story, sometimes leadership bios

**Optional, depending on shop size/scope:**
- Parts catalog
- Leasing page
- Platform-specific pages per aircraft model
- Government/military section
- Blog/news

**Gap in both competitors:** Neither has personality or warmth — no team photos, no real story, no testimonials. Nothing that feels human.

## Differentiation Opportunities for South Air Helicopters

1. **Personal/family-business warmth** — Mike Pike is a known, named person. Lean into being smaller and relationship-driven where Arrow and Summit feel corporate. Real photo, real story.
2. **NASA partnership** — Neither competitor has anything like this. Strong unique angle if details can be confirmed (which programs, how long).
3. **Focused single-location shop** — Positioning against sprawling multi-division competitors: you're not getting lost in a big company, you're working directly with a small trusted team at Pearland Regional Airport.
4. **Bell specialization as authority** — Own being *the* Bell specialists rather than competing on breadth across every manufacturer.
5. **Certifications need equal visibility** — FAA repair station number (XRIR622K) and Bell Customer Service Facility status should be as prominent as the NASA angle, since certs are currency in this industry.
